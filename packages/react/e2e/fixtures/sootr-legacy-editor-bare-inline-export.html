What you have to learn:
 - What are the main organs involved in the digestive system?
 - What is the sequence of organs food passes through during digestion?

You need to understand the organisation of the digestive system comprehensively to effectively manage patient nutrition, digestion-related disorders, and GI emergencies in nursing care. Knowing the sequence and types of organs involved will help you anticipate complications and support digestion-related interventions safely and informatively.

Digestive system components
Consists of two main groups of organs:
1) The alimentary canal (digestive canal or gastrointestinal tract)
2) The accessory digestive organs .

Alimentary canal:
- Continuous muscular tube approximately 5–7 meters long in living adults.
- Extends from the mouth (starting at the oral cavity) to the anus.
- This tube goes through the head, neck, thorax, abdomen, and pelvis.
- It includes the:
1) Mouth
2) Pharynx (throat)
3) Esophagus (food tube)
4) Stomach
5) Small intestine
6) Large intestine
7) Anal canal

Accessory digestive organs

- They do not form part of the continuous tube

- Essential for digestion by producing and delivering secretions into the alimentary canal via ducts.

- These organs physically or chemically assist digestion and mechanical processing.

- These include:

1) Teeth

2) Tongue

3) Salivary glands

4) Liver

5) Gallbladder

6) Pancreas


Upper vs. lower gastrointestinal tract:


Upper digestive tract:

- Mouth

- Pharynx

- Esophagus

- Stomach


Lower digestive tract:

- Small intestine

- Large intestine

- Anal canal




Nursing relevance:
 The organisation of the digestive system as a continuous functional unit highlights the need for comprehensive assessment of all components, from oral cavity to anus, including secretory organs. This knowledge guides interventions such as feeding support, aspiration precautions, monitoring nutrient absorption, and educating patients about digestion and nutrition in diverse care settings.
